1. Getting started: no cloud setup needed

YiKuaiJi helps you record spending for trips, households, couples and small groups. A ledger is a separate book of expenses. An entry is one expense. A draft is an entry you have not confirmed yet. Basic bookkeeping does not require a YiKuaiJi account, WebDAV or an API key.

  1. Open the Ledgers tab at the bottom. Open an existing ledger, or select New ledger. Give it a recognizable name, such as “Weekend trip”.
  2. Choose the base currency: the currency used to show the ledger's total spending. For example, choose CNY if you want totals in Chinese yuan. Do not change the whole ledger's base currency just to record one expense in US dollars.
  3. Open the ledger and add a fictional expense, such as “Lunch, CNY 20”. Check the date, currency and category, then select Confirm and save.
  4. Open Summary to check the result. Only saved entries count toward the totals. The current ledger and any filters also affect what you see.

Success check: the entry appears in the ledger and is still there after you reopen the app. You can now start recording expenses. Sharing and external processing, explained later, are optional.

2. Managing, switching and archiving ledgers

  1. The Ledgers tab is the home for all your ledgers. Create and open ledgers here. Open the ledger you want to work with before going to Settings or Summary; these pages use the current ledger.
  2. To tidy away a ledger you rarely use, open its card menu and select Archive. Archiving only hides it from this device's ledger home. Its entries, attachments and sync connection remain. Other members' ledger lists are not changed.
  3. Open Archived ledgers at the top right of the ledger home, find the ledger and restore it. You cannot archive your last unarchived ledger, so there is always an available working ledger.

Archiving is not deletion and does not stop syncing. To stop sharing from this device, disconnect the relevant ledger on its WebDAV page.

3. Manual entries, closing the editor and drafts

  1. Open a ledger and add an entry. Fill in the merchant, amount, currency, date, category and any notes you need.
  2. If you are not ready to save, go back normally or swipe down to close the editor. Your input is automatically saved as a draft. Do not force-quit the app instead of closing the editor normally: the last changes may still be lost if the system terminates the app, storage fails or the device has a fault.
  3. Return to the ledger's drafts to continue editing. Drafts do not count toward totals and are not synced to other devices as saved entries.
  4. Check the details, then select Confirm and save. When editing an existing entry, also confirm and save before checking the updated result.

Success check: your input can be reopened from a draft, and a saved entry appears after confirmation. Keep independent copies of important original receipts.

4. Taking or importing receipt photos

OCR means reading the letters and numbers in an image. It does not verify that the expense is correct.

  1. When adding an entry, choose to take a photo or import an image. Grant camera or photo-selection permission when the system first asks if you want to use that feature. You can still enter expenses manually if you decline, and change permissions later in system settings.
  2. Flatten the receipt and use good lighting. Include the merchant, date and total. Avoid glare, blur and cutting off the amount. Images may contain capture metadata; remove information you do not want to reveal before sharing them.
  3. After on-device recognition, check each field. Is the total the amount actually paid? Is the date correct? Is the currency right? Were refunds or discounts misread?
  4. If you want an external model to help organize the text, follow the DeepSeek setup chapters first, then explicitly choose Process with external API. On-device capabilities vary; not every device has a supported system model.
  5. Nothing is recorded as a saved entry until you select Confirm and save. If recognition fails, fill in the fields manually instead of repeatedly sending the same receipt.

5. Filters, multiple selection and spending summaries

  1. Open the filter next to Recent entries. You can select a single date or date range, a keyword and Payer (recorder). Conditions apply together, and a date range includes both its first and last day.
  2. Keep the filters in place, then use the selection button or press and hold an entry to select multiple entries. Select all and export only apply to the currently visible filtered results. Check your selection again after changing filters.
  3. In Summary, filter by date and recorder to view categories, merchants and daily or monthly trends. Drafts and entries in the recycle bin are excluded.

Important: Payer (recorder) currently relies mainly on the recorded author. It is not a verified identity or proof of who actually paid, and it does not establish expense splits, transfers or debts. Older entries may show Unlabeled recorder. Multi-currency totals use each entry's saved base-currency amount.

6. What WebDAV is, and whether you need it

WebDAV lets an app read and write files in a remote folder you specify. You can use a storage provider that supports WebDAV or your own server. Not every cloud drive supports it. If you only keep expenses on one device, you can skip it entirely.

WebDAV and an external model do different jobs. WebDAV stores and syncs encrypted entries and optional photos. An external model helps organize recognized text. Their accounts, passwords and purposes are separate.

Before starting, prepare four things: your provider's WebDAV HTTPS address, WebDAV username, dedicated app password and the name you want other ledger members to see. The storage service may charge fees. Read its pricing, privacy policy, storage-region information and access rules yourself.

Share only with trusted people. Encryption cannot prevent an authorized member from copying, changing or deleting shared content. The app does not currently provide per-member read-only permissions, a remove-member control or automatic key rotation.

7. WebDAV preparation: get the right details

  1. Sign in to your chosen storage provider's official website in a browser. Look for WebDAV, third-party apps, app passwords or security settings. Names and locations vary. If you cannot find them, check its official help and confirm that WebDAV is supported.
  2. Enable WebDAV if required. Follow the provider's instructions to create an app password specifically for YiKuaiJi. Think of it as a separate key for this app. Do not assume your website login password will work, and do not enter an SMS verification code.
  3. Copy the WebDAV HTTPS address specified by the provider. This is not its website login page, an ordinary file-sharing link or an address made by guessing a folder path.
  4. Where supported, restrict access to a dedicated folder, such as YiKuaiJi. Follow the provider's instructions about whether to append a subfolder to the address. If unsure, verify it in the provider's official client first. Do not grant access to your entire private drive and then give those credentials to others.
  5. Keep the correct username and app password in a password manager you trust. Do not send them to the developer or post screenshots in group chats.

Example format: https://dav.example.com/your-folder/ . This is only an example and will not work as a real service. Replace it with your own address. Use valid HTTPS; do not disable certificate checks to get around a connection error.

8. Creating a WebDAV share: the first device

  1. From Ledgers, open the ledger you want to share. Go to Settings → Configure sync. Check the ledger name at the top, then choose Create share.
  2. WebDAV HTTPS address: paste the full address supplied by your provider. Check for spaces, a missing https:// prefix or an accidentally pasted website link.
  3. WebDAV account: enter the username specified by your provider. Some providers use an email address; others use a separate username.
  4. WebDAV app password: paste the app password you just created. This is not a YiKuaiJi invite code or an external model's API key.
  5. My name: enter a name the other members will recognize, such as “Alex”. It will be used as shared member information; it does not have to be your legal name.
  6. Select Create and preview. The app contacts the server to check permissions, creates app folders if needed and reads remote state. Review each upload and download in the preview, then select Confirm sync. Canceling the preview does not undo the saved connection; disconnect separately if needed.
  7. Once WebDAV connected appears, you can select Save as WebDAV preset and give the preset a name. You can reuse its address and credentials for another ledger, but still need to create or join that ledger's share. Do not save presets on a public device.

Success check: there are no connection errors, and a sync result appears after you confirm. “Connected” alone does not prove that another device has received the data.

9. Joining an existing WebDAV ledger: the second device

  1. Ask the creator to select Copy invite code on the connected ledger's page and send it through a trusted private channel. A regular code contains the remote ledger identifier and decryption key, but not the WebDAV username or app password. It is not a TestFlight redemption code or proof of an app purchase.
  2. Create an empty ledger on the second device, using the same base currency as the original. Do not try to join through a ledger that already contains your own entries.
  3. Open the empty ledger, go to Settings → Configure sync and choose Join existing. Enter an HTTPS address, account and app password that can access the same remote folder. Enter your own name under My name.
  4. Paste the complete Share invite code, select Join and preview, review the downloads and confirm the sync.
  5. Add one fictional, non-private test entry on each device. Sync each device, then sync the other device and check that both entries arrive. Verify text syncing in both directions before enabling photos.

A quick invite is an optional shortcut. The creator selects Generate quick invite (includes credentials) and sets a 6–12 digit PIN. The recipient selects Import quick invite (includes credentials), pastes the text or scans the QR code, enters the PIN, checks the filled-in details and then joins. Tell the recipient the PIN separately, preferably spoken in person; do not send it in the same message as the invite.

A quick invite contains credentials that can be decrypted with the PIN. A numeric PIN provides limited protection: the invite is not single-use, individually revocable or automatically expiring. Do not publish it or forward it to strangers. Where the service allows it, prefer separate credentials for each member, limited to the dedicated folder.

10. WebDAV previews, automatic sync and photos

  1. To see a list before each sync, open the current ledger's WebDAV page and select Sync now. Read the uploads, downloads, deletions and photo items before confirming.
  2. The quick-sync button at the top right of the ledger page checks all connected ledgers. With no conflicts, it completes directly rather than showing a full list every time. Conflicting changes to the same field require a choice. A rotating icon means work is in progress; a brief green state indicates that run completed.
  3. To control each upload yourself, keep all switches under Settings → Automatic sync off and use the preview path above. Enabling sync when the app opens, after adding an entry or on an interval authorizes syncing under that condition. These options work only while the app is in the foreground; continuous background sync is not guaranteed.
  4. To share photos, enable Sync receipt images on the current ledger's WebDAV page and read the confirmation. Check the switch on every device that needs to send or receive photos. Existing receipt and product attachments linked to the ledger may also be synced, not just photos you take afterward.
  5. With photo sync off, entries still sync without images. If another member has not uploaded a photo, or a remote file is temporarily missing, text entries can still sync. A missing-photo notice does not mean the entry was lost. Ask a member who has the original image to enable photo sync and upload it, then try syncing your device again.

Turning photo sync off stops further transfers; it does not delete photos already uploaded. Sync is not an independent backup: mistaken edits or deletions may spread to all devices.

11. WebDAV troubleshooting

  1. 401/403: check the account, app password and dedicated-folder permissions with your provider. Being able to sign in on its website does not prove that WebDAV credentials or permissions are correct. If the app password is invalid, create a new dedicated password and update the connection.
  2. 404: during setup, check the complete address and folder. A notice after syncing that a photo was not uploaded or was skipped refers to a missing attachment. Do not delete the ledger because of that notice.
  3. 405/409: check that the service allows the app to create folders and that the path is a valid WebDAV folder. Do not repeatedly create new shares or empty remote folders as an experiment.
  4. 429 or quota errors: stop repeatedly pressing sync. Wait for rate limits to reset, or check the provider's storage capacity, request allowance and file-size limits.
  5. Timeouts or certificate errors: check your network, device time, server status and HTTPS certificate. Do not disable security checks. For decryption errors, verify that you used the invite code for this exact ledger. Do not try force upload as a guess.

Force upload local data is a high-risk repair tool: it can delete entries that exist only remotely. It is not a beginner's network troubleshooting step. Export your records, keep original attachments and agree with all members which copy is complete before considering it.

12. DeepSeek example: API keys and costs

An external model is an optional text-processing assistant, not a requirement for bookkeeping. DeepSeek is only a setup example. It does not imply a partnership, guarantee or exclusive arrangement with YiKuaiJi. You may use another compatible service you trust.

An API key lets the app access your model-service account, much like an app password. Website sign-in, a chat subscription and API usage credit should not be assumed to be the same thing. Check your provider's current account dashboard, terms and pricing. Do not buy or use shared keys from an unknown source.

  1. In your browser, open the official DeepSeek platform at https://platform.deepseek.com/ and register or sign in yourself.
  2. Read its terms, privacy policy and pricing. Check processing regions, whether requests are retained and whether they may be used for training. If these conditions are unclear or unsuitable, do not send real receipts.
  3. Find API Keys or key management in the platform, create a key specifically for YiKuaiJi and store it securely. If API credit is required, purchasing it is your decision. This guide does not require a top-up or promise free credit.
  4. Official API documentation: https://api-docs.deepseek.com/ . Dashboard labels and available models may change. Follow the provider's documentation and your account's actual permissions.

A leaked key may let someone else spend your account's allowance. Do not put keys in screenshots, notes, prompts or feedback. If you suspect a leak, revoke the key with the provider and create a new one.

13. DeepSeek example: fill in the app settings

  1. Open Settings → Smart processing → External LLM API. For your first setup, fill in the form directly. To keep an existing configuration while adding another, use New profile first, then edit that profile; do not overwrite one you still need.
  2. Under Provider preset, choose DeepSeek. Check that Endpoint is https://api.deepseek.com/chat/completions . Changing the preset may leave a previously customized address unchanged, so always check the domain.
  3. In API Key, paste the key you created. Do not add quotation marks, a “Bearer ” prefix or extra spaces. When editing a saved configuration, leaving this field empty keeps the existing key. An empty password field does not mean the saved key is missing.
  4. Select Fetch models. From Discovered models, choose a model that supports text chat and JSON output and that your account is allowed to use. Model name must be the provider's exact model ID, not a nickname you invent. If the service does not support listing models, enter the ID from its official documentation manually.
  5. Start with the defaults: Provider default for thinking mode, temperature 0, output limit 8000 and timeout 120 seconds. Leave other parameters alone at first. Temperature controls variation in results. An output limit is not a fixed price, and thinking mode can increase processing time and cost.
  6. Select Test connection, then Save after a successful result. Fetching models and testing the connection send your key to the chosen service to request its model list, but do not send your entries or receipts. Success proves only that authentication and the model-list endpoint work; it does not guarantee processing support, available credit or that the selected model will work.
  7. Return to Settings and check which profile is selected. If you have profiles for several services, check the selected one before each processing request.

Success check: you can save the profile and retrieve a model list. Next, use fictional text to test actual processing. Do not send a batch of private receipts just because the connection test succeeded.

14. Your first external processing request and common errors

  1. Prepare a fictional receipt image containing only a test merchant, date and total such as CNY 10. Do not include names, phone numbers, card numbers or other people's information. Run on-device recognition first.
  2. Select Process with external API. Check the service domain, model and thinking settings in the confirmation. Data is sent only if you agree. For a batch, the confirmation covers the selected batch; it is not repeated for every image.
  3. The request includes recognized text blocks and IDs, barcode/QR-code candidates, processing prompts and model parameters. It does not attach the original image. Text itself may contain sensitive information. If you do not want to send it, cancel and fill in the entry manually.
  4. When the result arrives, check the amount, currency, date, merchant and category before confirming and saving. Fluent model output is not a reason to skip verification.

401/403: check key validity and permissions. 404: check the endpoint and model ID. 429: check rate limits and credit; do not keep retrying. Timeout: try later or increase the timeout reasonably. Truncated response: consider increasing the output limit, reducing input or disabling unnecessary thinking. If parameters are incompatible, restore defaults and check the provider's documentation.

Canceling a task in the app does not guarantee that the provider immediately stops processing or will not charge for it. Remove the relevant profile to stop using it for future requests. To invalidate the key itself, also revoke it in the provider's dashboard.

15. Multiple receipts and background limits

  1. Import up to 50 images in a batch. The app creates a local draft queue.
  2. If an external model is configured, you can select Review all with LLM. Read and confirm the batch data-sharing notice first. A batch increases the number of requests and may increase costs.
  3. Settings → Smart processing → Batch review concurrency controls how many receipts are processed at once. The default is 2, and you can choose 1–4. Start with 1 if the service rate-limits requests or your connection is unstable.
  4. When processing finishes, open each draft, check it and save it yourself. Failed items retain their local images and drafts; they do not become saved entries automatically.

Keep the app in the foreground until processing finishes where possible. iOS allows only limited background execution. If the system terminates the app, unfinished tasks are not automatically resumed. Check the drafts after reopening, then decide which items to retry.

16. Exchange rates versus actual payments

  1. Under Settings → Exchange rates, automatic mode downloads and caches daily reference rates from the European Central Bank (ECB). This uses the network but does not upload your entries. Choose manual mode if you do not want automatic rate downloads.
  2. In manual mode, fill in the currency pairs needed by the current ledger and check the direction. For example, “how many Chinese yuan equal one US dollar” is not the same as its inverse.
  3. Saving an entry locks in its exchange rate. Later reference-rate updates do not change old entries automatically. The amount charged by a bank or payment provider, including fees, may differ from the reference value.
  4. If you really need to recalculate history, preview it before confirming. Automatic mode processes applicable entries in the most recent 30 calendar days; manual mode processes all applicable historical entries. This may affect several ledgers that have not been deleted. When no rate exists for a day, such as a weekend, an earlier available reference day is used.

The app calculates cross-rates from ECB data, modifying the source information. Reference rates are not a promise of an actual transaction rate or investment advice. Source: https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/policy_and_exchange_rates/euro_reference_exchange_rates/html/index.en.html . Usage terms: https://www.ecb.europa.eu/services/disclaimer/html/index.en.html .

17. Exporting and restoring before changing devices

  1. Open the correct ledger, then go to Settings → Export data. Choose a location and confirm the export when prompted. Repeat for each ledger you need.
  2. JSON preserves structured data for importing back into YiKuaiJi. CSV is convenient for viewing in a spreadsheet. Export files are readable text, contain private spending data and are not encrypted. Store them somewhere you control.
  3. Exports do not include photo files and are not a complete backup of the app. They cannot fully restore drafts, connection passwords, model keys or other app state. Keep original receipts, photos and necessary credentials separately and securely. Do not uninstall the app or erase your old device immediately after exporting.
  4. Before changing devices, test importing into a test ledger. Open the destination ledger, select Settings → Import data, choose a YiKuaiJi JSON or CSV file, review and confirm. The destination ledger must have the same base currency. Import merges records; it does not roll the whole app back to a previous moment.
  5. To export only some entries, filter first, select the entries, export and check the scope. Do not send private expenses to people who do not need them.

Success check: verify the amounts, dates, categories and any needed attachments on the new device before disposing of data on the old one. Do not treat WebDAV sync as an independent historical backup.

18. Deleting data, leaving a share and getting help safely

  1. Deleted entries first go to the recycle bin and can be restored before permanent cleanup. Items beyond the retention period, currently 30 days, may be permanently deleted during cleanup. Deletions and restorations of shared entries also sync.
  2. Permanently deleting a single shared entry attempts to remove its remote operation history and associated photos. This requires a network connection and write/delete permissions. An unfinished sync, provider backups and copies already held by members must not be assumed to have been erased.
  3. Disconnecting WebDAV, deleting a preset or turning off photo sync does not erase remote data. There is currently no one-click remote wipe for an entire shared ledger.
  4. If the whole remote ledger must be removed, first agree with all members and make any necessary backups. Disconnect all devices. The storage-folder owner should verify the exact dedicated ledger folder, then delete that folder and its recycle-bin/version-history copies through the provider and revoke the relevant credentials. If the folder cannot be identified confidently, ask the provider first. Never delete the entire drive or other ledgers.
  5. To remove a member's access, changing My name or disconnecting your own device is not enough. Revoke that member's server-side access or old credentials, and assess whether to create a new share with a new key. Copies already downloaded cannot be remotely taken back.
